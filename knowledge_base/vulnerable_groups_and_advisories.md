# Clinical & Epidemiological Protocols for Vulnerable Populations in High-Pollution Events

## 1. Vulnerability Cohorts & Specific Pathophysiology
- **Pediatric Cohort (Ages 0 - 14)**:
  - Higher minute ventilation relative to body weight compared to adults.
  - Developing airways and narrower bronchiolar lumen heighten bronchospasm risks.
  - Advisory: Relocate sports and physical education to indoor air-conditioned or filtered gymnasiums when AQI > 150. If AQI > 300, close outdoor recreation.

- **Geriatric Cohort (Ages 65+)**:
  - Decreased physiological pulmonary reserve and higher prevalence of subclinical atherosclerosis.
  - High PM2.5 exposure triggers autonomic nervous system imbalance, cardiac arrhythmias, and acute myocardial infarction.
  - Advisory: Keep essential medications (bronchodilators, antihypertensives) easily accessible. Avoid morning walking routines during thermal inversions (early morning dawn/fog).

- **Chronic Respiratory Patients (Asthma, COPD, Bronchiectasis)**:
  - Chronic baseline airway hyper-reactivity.
  - Advisory: Maintain adherence to baseline corticosteroid/inhaler regimens. Wear N95 respirator if stepping outside is mandatory. Monitor peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR).

- **Cardiovascular Patients (CAD, Heart Failure, Hypertension)**:
  - Systemic inflammatory cascade driven by circulating ultrafine particles increases blood viscosity and thrombotic risk.
  - Advisory: Avoid heavy physical exertion (running, cycling) in urban corridors.

## 2. Mitigation Strategies & Air Filtration Protocols
- Use True HEPA (High-Efficiency Particulate Air) filtration rated at 99.97% efficiency for 0.3-micron particles.
- Maintain room sealing during high traffic and peak stagnation hours (typically 6:00 AM - 10:00 AM and 7:00 PM - 11:00 PM).
- Supplement with indoor air-purifying plants (Areca Palm, Snake Plant, Peace Lily) for volatile organic compounds (VOCs).
- Hydration: Adequate fluid intake supports mucosal ciliary clearance of inhaled particulate matter.
