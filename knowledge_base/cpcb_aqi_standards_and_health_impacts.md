# Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) National Air Quality Index (NAQI) Standards
Source: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Government of India.

## 1. NAQI Category Definitions and Breakpoints
The Indian National AQI classifies air quality into six distinct bands based on 24-hour ambient concentrations:

1. **Good (AQI 0 - 50)**:
   - PM2.5: 0 - 30 ug/m3
   - PM10: 0 - 50 ug/m3
   - NO2: 0 - 40 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Minimal impact; pristine ambient condition.

2. **Satisfactory (AQI 51 - 100)**:
   - PM2.5: 31 - 60 ug/m3
   - PM10: 51 - 100 ug/m3
   - NO2: 41 - 80 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.

3. **Moderate (AQI 101 - 200)**:
   - PM2.5: 61 - 90 ug/m3
   - PM10: 101 - 250 ug/m3
   - NO2: 81 - 180 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Breathing discomfort to people with lungs, asthma and heart diseases.

4. **Poor (AQI 201 - 300)**:
   - PM2.5: 91 - 120 ug/m3
   - PM10: 251 - 350 ug/m3
   - NO2: 181 - 280 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.

5. **Very Poor (AQI 301 - 400)**:
   - PM2.5: 121 - 250 ug/m3
   - PM10: 351 - 430 ug/m3
   - NO2: 281 - 400 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Respiratory illness to people on prolonged exposure; severe effects on sensitive categories.

6. **Severe (AQI 401 - 500+)**:
   - PM2.5: > 250 ug/m3
   - PM10: > 430 ug/m3
   - NO2: > 400 ug/m3
   - Health Impact: Affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with existing diseases.

## 2. Municipal and Public Health Directives
- **For PM2.5 Dominant Spikes**: Use genuine N95/N99 certified respirators. Standard surgical or cloth masks provide negligible filtration against sub-micron particles.
- **For PM10 Dominant Spikes**: Wet mopping instead of dry sweeping; avoid proximity to construction zones or unpaved dust sources.
- **For Gaseous Spikes (NO2/SO2/CO)**: Avoid heavy vehicular corridors, idle engine zones, and poorly ventilated industrial zones.
