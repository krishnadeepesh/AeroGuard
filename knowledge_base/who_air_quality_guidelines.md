# World Health Organization (WHO) Global Air Quality Guidelines (AQG)
Reference: WHO Guidelines for ambient particulate matter, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide.

## 1. Key Air Pollutants & Physiological Mechanisms
### Particulate Matter (PM2.5 and PM10)
- **PM2.5 (Fine particles <= 2.5 micrometers)**: Capable of penetrating deep into the alveolar regions of the lungs and entering the systemic blood circulation. Long-term exposure increases mortality from cardiovascular disease, stroke, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and lung cancer. Short-term spikes trigger acute asthma attacks and coronary events.
- **PM10 (Coarse particles <= 10 micrometers)**: Typically deposits in the upper respiratory tract and trachea-bronchial tree, causing airway inflammation, chronic bronchitis, and exacerbating allergic rhinitis.

### Gaseous Pollutants
- **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)**: Primarily emitted from motor vehicle combustion and power plants. Increases bronchial hyper-responsiveness and respiratory infections in children.
- **Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)**: Emitted from industrial combustion of fossil fuels containing sulfur. Causes bronchoconstriction within minutes of inhalation, especially in asthmatics.
- **Carbon Monoxide (CO)**: High affinity for hemoglobin forming carboxyhemoglobin (COHb), reducing oxygen carrying capacity to vital organs. Symptoms include dizziness, headaches, and cardiac ischemia.
- **Ozone (O3)**: Secondary photochemical pollutant. Potent oxidant that damages lung epithelial cells, impairs pulmonary function, and triggers severe asthma episodes.

## 2. Recommended Protective Measures by Exposure Severity
- **AQI Good / Moderate**: Normal outdoor activity permitted. Individuals with severe chronic respiratory sensitivities should monitor local peak hours.
- **AQI Poor**: Vulnerable groups (children, elderly, pregnant women, asthmatics) should curtail prolonged outdoor exertion. Keep indoor spaces closed during peak rush hours.
- **AQI Very Poor**: All individuals should avoid strenuous outdoor physical exertion. Sensitive groups must remain indoors. Use high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filtration indoors. Wear certified particulate respirators (N95/FFP2) when stepping outside.
- **AQI Severe / Hazardous**: Medical alert status. Universal stay-at-home advisory for vulnerable populations. Strict cancellation of outdoor sports and educational activities. Continuous indoor air purification. Immediate medical attention for persistent coughing, wheezing, or chest tightness.
